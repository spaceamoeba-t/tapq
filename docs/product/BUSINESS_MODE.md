# Business Mode

**Status: Planned**

> **Stay in the conversation. Let AI handle the lookup.**

Business Mode is TapQ's next proposed product experience: private, real-time AI assistance during meetings, conferences, and professional conversations.

## The problem

Meetings move quickly. A speaker may mention an acronym, technical term, company, metric, or domain reference that one participant does not know.

The usual choices are awkward:

- interrupt the speaker,
- pull out a phone and search,
- ask a chatbot and lose the thread of the conversation,
- or ignore it and risk misunderstanding what comes next.

TapQ is exploring a different interaction: **keep looking at the people in the room, and get help privately through the AirPods already in your ears.**

## How it could work

When the user explicitly starts Business Mode, TapQ maintains the meeting context and listens for moments where assistance may be useful.

If an unfamiliar or important term appears, TapQ can privately ask:

> “Want a quick explanation of CAC payback?”

The user can:

- **nod** → hear a short explanation,
- **shake** → dismiss it,
- **ignore** → continue with no interruption,
- **ask a short follow-up** → get more context.

A useful explanation should reflect the conversation, not just a dictionary definition.

For example:

> “CAC payback is how long it takes to recover customer acquisition cost. Here, they are discussing whether growth is becoming more efficient.”

## After the meeting

With session capture enabled, TapQ could turn the same context into:

- a concise meeting summary,
- key decisions,
- action items,
- important topics,
- a contextual glossary,
- questions or concepts the user marked during the meeting.

The long-term goal is to help **during the meeting and after it**, rather than functioning only as a recorder.

## Designed to stay out of the way

Business Mode should be quiet by default.

A good meeting assistant should:

- make few, high-value interventions,
- ask before giving an unsolicited explanation,
- keep responses short,
- allow every prompt to be ignored,
- never make consequential decisions on behalf of the user.

## Recording and consent

Business Mode is planned around explicit session controls. Recording or transcription should never begin silently.

Users are responsible for obtaining any consent required by local law, company policy, or the meeting organizer. TapQ should make recording state visible and provide clear stop and deletion controls.

See [Privacy Principles](PRIVACY.md).

## Why Business Mode comes next

TapQ already demonstrates a private interaction loop between AI, AirPods, and subtle user input. Business Mode extends that loop from supervising software agents to navigating information-dense human conversations.

It is the next step toward TapQ's broader vision: **real-time AI that is available without demanding the user's screen.**
